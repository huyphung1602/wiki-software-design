# Naur: Implications

Naur draws out three major consequences of the Theory Building View: its relationship to programming method, the proper status of programmers, and the educational implications.

**On method.** A programming method is a set of work rules prescribing what to do, in what order, using what notations, producing what documents. On the Theory Building View, no such method can be right — because a theory has no inherent division into parts or ordering, it can only be developed through the individual intellectual activity of the programmer. This does not mean method has no value: Floyd's study of five different methods found that their real contribution is educational — familiarity with model solutions, description techniques, and structuring principles shapes the quality of the theory a programmer builds. But which techniques to use and in what order must remain a matter for the individual programmer to decide, not a prescribed method.

**On programmer status.** The dominant view treats programmers as components in an industrial production process — easily replaceable, controllable by rules, requiring only brief education. The Theory Building View demands abandoning this notion. Since the theory is held by individuals and cannot be transferred by documentation alone, programmers are more like engineers or lawyers: professionals whose value rests on intellectual proficiency built over time. They need permanent positions and status commensurate with that responsibility.

**The editor's commentary** connects Naur's ideas to Extreme Programming. Kent Beck's notion of metaphor as a theory — "this program looks like an assembly line" — is exactly Naur's idea: two words that let later programmers guess correctly about structure. Without a shared metaphor, parallel teams decay into incoherence; with one, they add code in ways that fit together. On documentation, the editor's advice: document to help the next programmer build a theory, not to describe the code. Three minimal documentation items — the metaphors, the purpose of each major component, and drawings of major interactions — take a team far toward constructing a useful theory.
