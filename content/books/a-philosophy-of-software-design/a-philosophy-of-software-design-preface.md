# A Philosophy of Software Design — Preface

Part of [[a-philosophy-of-software-design]]

John Ousterhout wrote this book from his experience teaching CS 190 at Stanford, a software design course built around iterative code reviews. The book is an opinion piece — every problem described is one he experienced personally, every technique one he has used successfully.

## Key Points

- Software design — specifically [[problem-decomposition]] — is the central challenge in programming, yet it is barely taught in universities
- The state of the art has not progressed much beyond David Parnas' 1972 paper on module decomposition
- Design skill is what separates great programmers from average ones, and it can be taught through practice (not innate talent)
- The book's overarching goal: reduce [[complexity]]
- The approach is [[strategic-programming|strategic]] — invest in design upfront rather than just making code work
- Principles are high-level and philosophical (e.g., "define errors out of existence")
- Students learn best by writing code, making mistakes, and seeing how fixes relate to principles
