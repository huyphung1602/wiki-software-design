# Design Investment

Ousterhout's practical rule: spend **10-20% of total development time** on design investments. This is small enough not to significantly impact schedules, but large enough to produce compounding benefits over time.

Investments take two forms:
- **Proactive**: Taking extra time to find a simpler design, trying alternative approaches, imagining future changes, writing good documentation
- **Reactive**: Fixing design problems when you discover them instead of patching around them

The economics: initial projects take 10-20% longer, but within a few months you develop at least 10-20% faster than you would have tactically. Past benefits cover the cost of future investments — they become "free." Payback period is estimated at 6-18 months.

The key is **consistency**. Don't defer design improvements to "after the crunch" — there will always be another crunch. Continuous small investments are more effective than occasional large ones. The longer you wait to address design problems, the bigger and more intimidating they become.
